Welford's Recurrence Formulas for Online Mean/Variance and Inferential Statistics Analysis

Ex. 1 – Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

1. Derivation of Mean Recurrence (Knuth's Formula)

Let $\bar{x}_k$ be the sample mean of $k$ observations $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k$. By definition:
$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i = \frac{1}{k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k \right) = \frac{(k-1)\bar{x}_{k-1} + x_k}{k}$$ Rewriting gives Knuth's online mean update:
$$\bar{x}_k = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$$

2. Derivation of Sum of Squares (M_k or SS_k) Recurrence

Let $M_k = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2$ be the sum of squared differences from the mean. Notice that:
$$x_i - \bar{x}_k = x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1} - (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})$$ Summing over $i=1 \dots k$ and simplifying cross-terms yields Welford's key recurrence formula:
$$M_k = M_{k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - \bar{x}_k)$$ From $M_k$, the sample variance is simply $s_k^2 = \frac{M_k}{k - 1}$ (unbiased) or $\sigma_k^2 = \frac{M_k}{k}$ (biased).

3. Core Implementation (3 Lines of Pure Logic)

The online algorithm avoids numerical catastrophic cancellation when calculating variance over streaming data:

// Core Welford logic inside a streaming loop over value x_k at step k:
let delta = x_k - mean;
mean += delta / k;
M += delta * (x_k - mean); // M_k = M_{k-1} + (x_k - mean_{k-1}) * (x_k - mean_k)

Ex. 2 – Sampling Mean and Variance

Simulate $m$ hackers attempting to penetrate $n$ systems. Each penetration score is drawn from a discrete uniform distribution over integers $0, 1, \dots, 10$.

Theoretical Distribution Parameters ($0 \dots 10$ Uniform):
$$\mu = \frac{0 + 10}{2} = 5.0, \quad \sigma^2 = \frac{(10 - 0 + 1)^2 - 1}{12} = \frac{120}{12} = 10.0$$


Statistical Interpretation & Conclusions

  • Unbiased Estimators (LLN Effect):
    The average of the $m$ sampling means ($\bar{\bar{x}}$) converges to the theoretical mean $\mu = 5.0$. Similarly, the average of the unbiased sampling variances ($\bar{s}^2$, divided by $n-1$) converges precisely to the theoretical variance $\sigma^2 = 10.0$. This demonstrates empirically that the sample mean and sample variance are unbiased estimators of the population parameters.
  • Why this matters in Inferential Statistics:
    In real-world security or experimental contexts, we rarely know the true population parameters $(\mu, \sigma^2)$. Welford's algorithm allows computing reliable, unbiased sample estimates online without storing past data points in memory.
  • Effect of Increasing $n$ (Systems Attempted):
    As $n$ increases, the variance of the sample means across the $m$ hackers decreases proportionally to $\sigma^2 / n$ (Standard Error of the Mean). Similarly, the sampling variances become tightly clustered around $\sigma^2 = 10.0$. Larger sample sizes per trajectory yield far more precise estimates of the underlying population parameters.
